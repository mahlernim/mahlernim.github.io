Barriers and opportunities in biobank utilization: insights from a 3-year repeated cross-sectional survey of the Female Breast and Genital Disease with Microbiome Biobank Network in South Korea.

Ahn S, Cho HJ, Kang MS, Seo AN, Kim L, Choi KU, Roh MS, Kim EY

J Yeungnam Med Sci

한국어 요약

이 연구는 2022~2024년 한국 여성 유방 및 생식기 질환 마이크로바이옴 바이오뱅크 네트워크(FDMNet)의 연구자 설문을 통해 바이오뱅크 이용 양상, 접근 장벽, 연구 수요 및 마이크로바이옴 연구 관심을 조사했다. 5개 참여 대학병원 직원 155명의 자료를 분석한 결과, 바이오뱅크 자원 이용 경험은 비교적 높았지만 참여율과 기관 간 협력이 감소했으며, 복잡한 접근 절차와 절차 지식 부족이 주요 장벽으로 확인되었다.

English summary

This study used annual surveys conducted from 2022 to 2024 to examine biobank utilization patterns, access barriers, research needs, and interest in microbiome research within South Korea’s Female Breast and Genital Disease with Microbiome Biobank Network (FDMNet). Among 155 staff respondents from five participating university hospitals, biobank use was relatively high, but participation and interinstitutional collaboration declined, while complex access procedures and insufficient process knowledge were the leading barriers.

Key points

  • Online questionnaires were administered annually from 2022 through 2024. Quantitative data were summarized descriptively, and qualitative responses were analyzed with Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection and natural language processing to identify thematic clusters of user challenges.
  • Although 76% of respondents had engaged with biobank resources, participation rates and interinstitutional collaboration decreased, particularly in 2024. The study described this pattern in the context of the nationwide healthcare crisis.
  • The main barriers were complex access procedures (31.0%), insufficient knowledge of the process (23.9%), and concerns about Institutional Review Board approval (11.6%).
Audience
Biobank managers, biomedical data researchers, clinical investigators, research-ethics and data-governance professionals, and graduate students
Scope and limitations
The study was based on an online repeated cross-sectional survey of 155 respondents from five university hospitals, which limits generalizability to other institutions or national biobank systems. Because the analyses were descriptive and thematic, the findings do not establish causal relationships between the reported barriers and changes in participation or collaboration.
Research context
The study connects thematically with medical AI and data science through its focus on biobank operations, biomedical data access, natural-language-processing-based analysis of qualitative feedback, and clinical data integration.
