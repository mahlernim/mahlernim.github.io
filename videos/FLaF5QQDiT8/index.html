English summary

This video presents a structured and ethical workflow for integrating large language models such as ChatGPT and GPT-4 into academic paper writing. Its central principle is that authors should first write Version 0.1 from their own ideas and reasoning, and then use language models selectively for grammar and academic style polishing, critical review, and simulated peer review.

Key points

  • Using an entire draft generated by a language model and changing only some wording is described as unethical and potentially plagiarism because it may not represent the author's original thinking or voice.
  • Authors are advised to build an outline first and write a rough zero draft from their own notes, key concepts, and preliminary sentences.
  • At the outlining stage, prompts can specify a persona, task, context, and language requirement. The outline can then be iteratively revised by merging sections and expanding hierarchical subsections. The demonstration also shows how Obsidian can be used to connect literature, notes, and mind-map structures.
Audience
Academic researchers, medical educators, university students, and scholarly writers who seek to use language models without plagiarism while preserving authorial perspective and voice.
Scope and limitations
This material summarizes a video-based example and demonstration of ChatGPT-assisted academic writing. The medical education manuscript used as an example was presented as an instructional model and practical framework, and it did not formally measure student learning outcomes. Therefore, the source alone cannot establish the effectiveness, reproducibility, or reduction of plagiarism risk associated with the proposed workflow.
